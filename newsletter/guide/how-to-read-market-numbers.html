How to read your market numbers

Six numbers tell the story of a housing market. Here is what each one means, what it tells a buyer, what it tells a seller, and a plain way to say it out loud.

Three rules before you start
  • The numbers look back. They tell you what already happened. They never tell you what will happen next. So you say "prices were up 2% from last year," never "prices are going up."
  • Up is not "good" and down is not "bad." A higher price is good news for a seller and harder news for a buyer. That is why your email shows ▲ and ▼ in the same plain color, with no red or green.
  • Compare to last YEAR first. Housing has seasons. Spring is busy and winter is slow, every year. Comparing August to July mostly shows the season. Comparing August to last August shows the real change.

The six numbers

1. Median sold price

TrendVision calls it: Median Sold Price / Median Sale Price

Line up every home that sold this month from cheapest to most expensive. The one in the middle is the median. Half sold for more. Half sold for less.

We use the median, not the average, because one $5 million sale can drag an average way up. The median doesn't budge.

For a buyerWhat a "middle of the road" home cost here last month.
For a sellerThe direction of prices. It is NOT the value of their home. That takes a real pricing review.
Say it like this: "Half the homes in Placer County sold for more than $692,500 last month, and half sold for less. That's about 3% higher than a year ago."

2. New listings

TrendVision calls it: New Listings

How many homes came on the market this month. This is the fresh supply.

For a buyerMore new listings = more choices coming.
For a sellerMore new listings = more homes to compete with.
Say it like this: "About 1,400 homes came on the market in Sacramento County last month. That's more than last August, so buyers have more to look at."

3. Pendings (homes that went pending)

TrendVision calls it: Pending Sales / Under Contract

A home goes "pending" when the seller accepts an offer. It hasn't closed yet. Closed sales are 30 to 45 days old by the time we see them. Pendings are what buyers did this month. It is the freshest number you have.

For a buyerMore pendings = more buyers are out writing offers.
For a sellerMore pendings = buyers are active right now.
Say it like this: "Fewer homes went under contract this month than last month. Buyers slowed down a little."

4. Days on market

TrendVision calls it: Median Days on Market (DOM)

How many days a typical home took to get an accepted offer, counted from the day it was listed.

For a buyerLow number (under about 2 weeks) = decide fast. Higher number = more time to think and more room to ask for things.
For a sellerHow long to plan for. If their home sits much longer than this number, price or condition is usually why.
Say it like this: "A typical home took about 25 days to sell. That's 3 days faster than a year ago."

5. Months of inventory

Also called: months of supply, unsold inventory index

If no new homes were listed starting today, how many months would it take to sell everything that's for sale right now? It is homes for sale divided by homes sold per month. This one number tells you who has the edge.

Under 3 · sellers' edge3 to 6 · balancedOver 6 · buyers' edge

This is a rule of thumb, not a hard line. Different experts draw the lines in slightly different spots.

For a buyerLow = fewer choices and more competition. High = more choices and more room to negotiate.
For a sellerLow = less competition. High = price and condition matter more.
Say it like this: "There's about 3 months of homes for sale in Sacramento County. That's right on the line between a sellers' market and a balanced one."

6. Sale-to-list % (sold price vs. asking price)

TrendVision calls it: Sale Price to List Price Ratio / SP/LP %

What homes sold for, compared to what the seller was asking. 100% means sellers got their asking price on average. 102% means buyers paid 2% over asking. 97% means sellers took 3% less.

For a buyerOver 100% = expect competition. Under 100% = there is usually room to negotiate.
For a sellerA reality check on pricing. It usually compares to the last asking price, after any price cuts.
Say it like this: "On average, sellers got about 99% of their asking price."

Reading them together

If you see…It usually means…
Days on market going up + months of inventory going upThe market is slowing down. Buyers have more time and more choices.
Days on market going down + sale-to-list over 100%The market is speeding up. Homes are getting more than one offer.
New listings up, pendings flatMore supply than demand right now. Keep an eye on months of inventory in the next report.
Price jumps or drops a lot in one month in a small areaProbably just which homes sold that month. Check the number of sales. Under about 50 sales, don't read much into one month. (This is why El Dorado County jumps around more.)

Words to use, words to skip

SkipWhySay instead
"Prices will go up / rates will drop"It's a prediction. Nobody knows, and the DRE and NAR rules say don't promise outcomes."Prices were up 2% from last year."
"Now is a great time to buy"Advice dressed up as fact."Buyers have more choices than last year."
"Lock your rate now"You're not a lender."Rates rose this week. A loan officer can tell you what that means for you."
"This area is improving / up-and-coming / safe / family-friendly"Fair Housing. Never describe a neighborhood's people or character.Talk about the homes and the numbers: price, days on market, what's for sale.
